# Data partnership & attribution outreach — drafts

Send from `hello@nadc.info`. Each is written to be sent as-is, but skim and
personalize the bracketed bits. Suggested order: FracTracker first (clearest
ask, mission-aligned), then DataCenterWatch, then Cleanview.

A note on posture used in all three: we ask **permission before use**, we lead
with what we offer them (attribution, traffic, a two-way tip pipeline), and we
are upfront that NADC takes donations and sells organizing materials — so
"non-commercial" terms genuinely matter to us. That honesty is the brand.

## 1. FracTracker Alliance — data-sharing request

**To:** jones@fractracker.org
**Subject:** Data-sharing request from Neighbors Against Data Centers (nadc.info)

Hi Ted,

I'm Kevin Rizer, founder of Neighbors Against Data Centers (nadc.info) — a
nationwide, fact-checked hub that helps residents understand and organize
around data center proposals in their communities. We maintain a national
tracker of proposed, contested, and resolved projects, each entry carrying its
sources and a verification label, plus a tip line that routes neighbors'
reports through editorial review.

Your US Data Centers Tracker is, as far as we can tell, the most complete
picture anyone has assembled of this buildout — and your invitation for
collaboration is why I'm writing rather than just admiring it.

**The ask:** we'd like permission to incorporate FracTracker's data center
data into our tracker. I want to be straightforward about why permission
matters: NADC accepts donations and sells yard signs and organizing materials
to fund the work, so we don't qualify as non-commercial under your current
terms, and we won't use the data without your okay.

**What we'd offer in return:**
- Prominent attribution on every entry derived from your data (worded however
  you prefer — we can also link your donation page from those entries).
- A two-way pipeline: our tip line surfaces brand-new proposals from residents,
  often before they hit the news. We'd be glad to share verified tips back to
  FracTracker on whatever cadence works.
- Corrections: anything our community flags as wrong or stale, we'd pass along.

If a license fee or data-sharing agreement is the right structure, we're open
to that conversation too. And if parts of the dataset are off-limits, we'd
welcome whatever subset you're comfortable sharing.

Happy to get on a call. Thanks for the work you do — it made our map of this
problem possible to imagine.

Kevin Rizer
Founder, Neighbors Against Data Centers
nadc.info · hello@nadc.info

## 2. Data Center Watch — reuse & attribution

**To:** [contact via datacenterwatch.org / their Substack]
**Subject:** Attribution & reuse of Data Center Watch research — nadc.info

Hi,

I'm Kevin Rizer, founder of Neighbors Against Data Centers (nadc.info), a
fact-checked national hub for residents facing data center proposals. Your
reports and weekly briefings are the best running record of contested projects
anywhere, and several entries in our public tracker already cite
datacenterwatch.org as a source, the same way we'd cite a news outlet.

I'm writing to ask two things:

1. **How would you like to be attributed?** We want to credit you the way you
   prefer — organization name, link format, per-entry citation versus a
   sources page, whatever you'd like.
2. **Would you be open to a more structured arrangement?** Today we cite your
   public reports entry-by-entry. If you'd be willing to share data in a more
   systematic way (even a periodic spreadsheet), we'd attribute every derived
   entry and share back what our tip line surfaces — residents frequently
   report proposals before they're covered anywhere. We're tracking the same
   fights from the same side, and it seems wasteful to duplicate effort.

For transparency: NADC takes donations and sells organizing materials (yard
signs, door hangers) to fund the research — so if any of your material carries
non-commercial expectations, tell us and we'll respect that line.

Thanks for the briefings — they're consistently the first place we learn that
a fight has started.

Kevin Rizer
Founder, Neighbors Against Data Centers
nadc.info · hello@nadc.info

## 3. Cleanview — dataset licensing inquiry (paid is on the table)

**To:** [contact/sales via cleanview.co]
**Subject:** Licensing inquiry: US data center dataset for nadc.info

Hi,

I'm Kevin Rizer, founder of Neighbors Against Data Centers (nadc.info), a
national, fact-checked tracker of proposed and contested data center projects,
built for residents and local officials. Your US data center map is one of the
most complete views of the buildout we've seen, and I'd like to ask about
licensing.

**What we're looking for:**
- Ongoing access (API or periodic export) to your US data center dataset —
  locations, status (proposed / under construction / operating), developer,
  and capacity where you have it.
- Rights to display the data on nadc.info with attribution, alongside our own
  verified research. We label every entry on our tracker with its source and
  confidence level, so your data would be clearly credited wherever it
  appears.

**What I'd like to know:**
1. Do you license this dataset, and what does pricing look like for a small
   advocacy organization? (We're an LLC funded by small donations and
   merchandise — think "nonprofit budget" regardless of the paperwork.)
2. How would you want attribution handled?
3. If full licensing isn't a fit, is there a tier — even a lead-style feed of
   new project announcements — that is?

We're a paying-customer prospect, not a scraper: we haven't ingested any of
your data and won't without an agreement.

Thanks — happy to do a quick call if easier.

Kevin Rizer
Founder, Neighbors Against Data Centers
nadc.info · hello@nadc.info

## Where the aggregator articles fit (the four links you sent)

Business Insider, Visual Capitalist, and Axios are *journalism about* datasets
(mostly Cleanview's and DC Byte's) — they're lead lists and context, not
licensable sources themselves. Anything we take from them gets re-verified
against a primary source and cited to that source, exactly like the seed data.
